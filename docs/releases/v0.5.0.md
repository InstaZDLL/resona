# Resona 0.5.0 — Apple Music and Dolby Atmos

Resona now records Apple Music as well as Spotify, in lossless up to 24-bit/48 kHz. With a virtual cable set up as 7.1, it also keeps Apple Music's Dolby Atmos in surround, as 5.1 or 7.1 FLAC.

## New

- **Apple Music.** Choose the app in Settings → Recording → **Music app**. Resona follows Apple Music for Windows through Windows' media controls (its window title never changes), splits its tracks, and tags them from Deezer. Its audio comes from Apple Music's library agent (`AMPLibraryAgent.exe`), which Resona finds by itself.
- **Lossless up to 24-bit/48 kHz.** Apple Music's Lossless tracks come out bit-perfect when nothing alters them on the way. In Apple Music:
  - turn on *Lossless audio* and set *Streaming* to Lossless; the switch alone keeps AAC 256;
  - set the volume to maximum;
  - turn off Sound Check, Sound Enhancer, crossfade and Dolby Atmos.

  Then set the output device to the track's rate (44.1 or 48 kHz, shown in Apple Music's *Lossless* badge), and turn off any headset equalizer. Each saved track reports its fidelity from a sample-by-sample analysis of the capture, including whether it appears bit-perfect. The settings window lists these steps.
- **Dolby Atmos in surround.** Turn on Settings → Audio → **Keep Dolby Atmos in surround**, set VB-Cable's *CABLE Input* to *7.1 Surround* in Windows, and set Dolby Atmos to *Always On* in Apple Music.
  - **Files:** Atmos tracks are saved with their surround channels in FLAC or WAV, 24-bit. Channels that stay silent for the whole track are left out when the rest is a standard layout: a 7.1 render with a silent side pair is saved as 5.1. The files keep their speaker layout: WAV in its channel mask, FLAC in a metadata field that compatible players read.
  - **MP3:** a stereo downmix of the surround channels, the LFE left out.
  - **Lossy source:** Atmos is lossy (Dolby Digital Plus); this is Apple Music's render of it, not a lossless source.
  - **Cable only:** a headset set up as 7.1 still gets stereo from Apple Music, even with surround on in its own software.
- **The virtual cable works with Apple Music.** It takes the rate of the device Apple Music was playing on. Apple Music keeps that rate when it moves to the cable, so a cable at another rate would resample every track. If the rate cannot be matched, Apple Music stays on its device and Resona says so.

> [!NOTE]
> With Dolby Atmos set to *Always On*, Apple Music plays the Atmos version of every track that has one, never its Lossless one. Record Atmos and Lossless in separate sessions, switching the setting in between.

> [!TIP]
> Apple Music cannot change rate while it plays: when you switch between 44.1 and 48 kHz tracks, change the device's rate before you start recording, not during it.

## Changed

- **Messages and quality labels name the app recorded:** Spotify or Apple Music. Switching the app clears the warnings that belonged to the other one.
- **Library updates**, Slint 1.19 among them. Resona is now built with Rust 1.100.

## Security

- **URL parsing.** The URL library Resona uses through Slint and its HTTP client could overflow the stack on nested `blob:` URLs ([RUSTSEC-2026-0592](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2026-0592)). Resona never asks such a URL for its origin; the library is updated anyway.

## Install

Download **`resona_0.5.0_x64-setup.exe`** below. Its SHA-256 checksum is in `SHA256SUMS.txt`. The installer runs for the current user without administrator privileges.

> [!NOTE]
> The installer is signed with a self-signed certificate, so Windows may still show a SmartScreen warning.
