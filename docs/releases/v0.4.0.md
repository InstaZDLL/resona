# Resona 0.4.0 — Cuts at the catalogue length

This release places every cut where the track really ends: complete tracks now come out at Spotify's catalogue length within a few milliseconds in testing, without borrowing audio from the track before or after them.

## Fixed

- **Spotify's half-second gap between tracks.** In normal non-gapless transitions between albums, Spotify inserts 500 ms of digital silence that belongs to neither track. Resona gave it to one track or the other, so files came out up to 0.8 s longer than the catalogue. It is now left out of both files.
- **Albums played in order.** Within an album, Spotify plays the next track right after the last one, without that half second. Tracks that end on their own silence, as on live albums, now keep all of it instead of losing half a second.
- **Skips.** After a skip, the next file could open on the fade-out of the track you skipped, or lose up to 150 ms of its own start. The cut now lands on the moment Spotify switches tracks.
- **Gapless transitions and quiet endings.** When one track flows into the next without silence, or ends on a quiet background hiss, the cut now goes to the track's start plus its catalogue length, which held to the millisecond in testing.

Measured with Spotify Lossless on a playlist, a live album and skips within and across albums: every complete track came out within 21 ms of its catalogue length, most of them to the millisecond. Tracks recorded before 0.4.0 are not changed.

> [!NOTE]
> Spotify shows lengths two ways: playlist pages round them to the nearest second, album pages cut them down. Resona rounds, like playlists, so a file of exactly 5:26.5 reads 5:27 in Resona and 5:26 on the album page.

> [!TIP]
> With **Crossfade** or **Automix** on, Spotify plays the end of one track over the start of the next, and the tracks involved lose the length of the overlap. Resona warns when it sees it: turn them off in Spotify → Settings → Playback for complete tracks.

The way cuts are placed adapts the technique of [Spytify](https://github.com/jwallet/spy-spotify)'s Go rewrite (MIT), credited in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.
